== Availability SLI (time based)
\var{SLI} \% = \frac{\text{Total time} - \text{Downtime}}{\text{Total time}} \times 100
? The share of the time the service was up, as a percentage.
? Take the downtime from the total, then divide by the total.
> A 30-day window is 43,200 minutes. The service was down for 25.
= \var{SLI} = \frac{43200 - 25}{43200} \times 100 = 99.94
== Request based SLI
\var{SLI} \% = \frac{\text{good requests}}{\text{valid (total) requests}} \times 100
? The share of requests that went well. Busy periods weigh more than quiet ones.
> 9,990 good requests out of 10,000.
= \var{SLI} = \frac{9990}{10000} \times 100 = 99.9
== Error budget (percentage)
\text{Error budget} \% = 100\% - \var{SLO} \%
? If the target is not 100%, the gap is how much failure you are allowed.
? Teams can spend it on risky changes while some is left.
> A target of 99.9% leaves a budget of 0.1%.
== Allowed downtime
\text{Allowed downtime} = (1 - \var{SLO}) \times \text{window length}
~ For example 0.001 × 43,200 min = 43.2 min
? Turn the budget percentage into minutes: the allowed fraction times the length of the window.
> 99.9% over 43,200 minutes.
= \var{allowed} = 0.001 \times 43200 = 43.2
== Budget remaining / consumed
\text{Remaining} = \text{Allowed} - \text{Actual downtime}
\text{Consumed} \% = \frac{\text{Actual}}{\text{Allowed}} \times 100
? Remaining: what you may still spend. Consumed: the share already used.
> 43.2 minutes allowed and 25 used. 18.2 remain.
= \var{consumed} = \frac{25}{43.2} \times 100 = 57.87
== Burn rate
\text{Burn rate} = \frac{\text{fraction of budget consumed}}{\text{fraction of window elapsed}} = \frac{\text{actual error rate}}{\text{allowed error rate}}
? How fast the budget is going compared with an even pace.
? Above 1 means it will run out before the window ends.
> 57.9% of the budget is gone after 50% of the window.
= \var{burn} = \frac{57.9}{50} = 1.16
== Time until budget exhausted
\text{Time to exhaustion} = \frac{\text{window}}{\text{burn rate}}
~ A burn rate of 1 is exactly on budget.
? At an even pace (burn rate 1) the budget lasts exactly the window. Burning twice as fast halves that.
> A 30-day window with burn rate 2 runs out in 15 days (counted from the start of the window).
== SLO met?
\text{Met if } \var{SLI} \ge \var{SLO}
\text{or equivalently } \text{Downtime} \le \text{Allowed downtime}
? SLI is what happened, SLO is the target. You pass if what happened is at least the target.
? Saying downtime is within the allowance means the same thing.
> The service measured 99.94% against a 99.9% target, so it is met. 25 minutes is within 43.2.
== Cyclomatic complexity
\var{CC} = E - N + 2P
? It counts the independent paths through code, drawn as a graph of steps (N) and arrows (E).
? More paths means more tests needed and harder code to follow. P is the number of separate graphs, usually 1.
> 9 arrows, 8 steps, one function.
= \var{CC} = 9 - 8 + 2 \times 1 = 3
== Line coverage
\text{Line coverage} = \frac{\text{lines run}}{\text{executable lines}} \times 100
? The share of the code's lines that your tests ran at least once.
> 3 of 5 lines run is 60%.
== Branch coverage
\text{Branch coverage} = \frac{\text{outcomes taken}}{\text{all outcomes}} \times 100
? Each decision has a true and a false way out. This is the share of those your tests took.
> 2 of 4 outcomes is 50%.
== MTBF as this paper defines it
\var{MTBF} = \frac{\sum (\var{MTTD} + \var{MTTR})}{n}
? For each incident add the time to detect and the time to repair, then average.
> Totals of 6, 1.5 and 10 hours average 5.83 hours.
== MTTD and MTTR
\var{MTTD} = \frac{\sum (\text{detected} - \text{start})}{n}
\var{MTTR} = \frac{\sum (\text{restored} - \text{detected})}{n}
? How long, on average, to notice a problem, and then to fix it.
> Waits of 40, 5 and 90 minutes give a mean of 45.
